# NutriLog Goes Public in Three Careful Stages

NutriLog can grow from Jan's personal test app into a public free plus premium service without a rewrite, provided a handful of hard to change choices are made before the first outside person signs up: a server in the EU (Supabase in Frankfurt fits best), an own web address instead of GitHub Pages, accounts with email code sign-in, the Claude key moved from the browser onto that server, an 18+ age limit, and a recipe archive that keeps recipe facts and links instead of downloaded Instagram media. Almost everything in Jan's ideas 1 to 8 (more sex options, simple and advanced goals and activity, explained macro and meal splits, a diet profile without overlaps, the weekly supplement plan and the quick photo log screen) is screen work and local calculation that can be built now on the current app and carries over unchanged; only premium locks, usage limits, PC and phone sync and the redesigned chat should wait for the server, so they are built once. Servers are cheap (about 0 USD a month now, about 25 to 30 USD in a closed beta, about 50 to 110 USD at 10,000 users, all estimates), so the real money question is Claude usage: a typical premium user costs about 1.9 USD a month with today's prompts and about 0.65 USD with leaner ones (estimates), against roughly 32 EUR a year of net revenue at a 39.99 EUR annual price. Legally, nearly everything NutriLog stores counts as health data under the GDPR, which means explicit consent, an impact assessment and EU hosting, and the app must stay a wellness tool (no "deficiency" or treatment claims) to avoid becoming a regulated medical device; for the same reason the supplement feature should track and warn rather than prescribe. AI photo estimates are typically off by about a third and usually too low, so the quick photo log must be built for fast correction. Legal, tax and medical points in this report are research findings, not professional advice.

## (c) Key findings in plain words

- **Servers are cheap; Claude is the real cost.** About 0 USD a month for you alone, about 25 to 30 USD in a beta, about 50 to 110 USD at 10,000 users (estimates). A typical premium user costs about 1.9 USD a month in Claude usage with today's prompts, about 0.65 USD with leaner prompts (estimates).
- **Without limits, heavy users lose money.** A very active user on today's prompts would cost about 72 EUR a year against about 32 EUR of net income (estimate). The server must cap each person's monthly usage.
- **Your data format was built well.** Stable ids and "deleted" markers mean sync can be added to the current app instead of rewriting it.
- **Moving the web address later hurts.** Browsers keep app data per address; a later move empties everyone's local storage and installs. Pick the address before anyone else installs.
- **GitHub Pages is fine for you, not for a paid service.** GitHub's terms exclude commercial software services.
- **Almost everything NutriLog stores is health data under EU law** (allergies, weight over time, food logs linked to goals, supplement intake, "low in" flags). That means explicit consent, an impact assessment, EU hosting and a privacy notice.
- **Stay a wellness app.** Words like "deficiency", "treat", "prevent a disease" or "patient" can turn the app into a medical device that needs certification you could not realistically get.
- **Supplement suggestions are the riskiest feature.** Tracking what people already take, with safety warnings, is safe; proposing new supplements and doses is where legal and health risk sits.
- **Photo estimates are rough.** In studies, AI photo estimates were off by about 36 % on average and usually too low, most of all for fat and large portions. Mistaking the dish (eggs read as pasta) is the biggest single error, so the dish name must be easy to check.
- **The hand helps only if its size is known**, and no study has measured how much it helps an AI. Your own weighed test is the honest way to find out.
- **One tap question about cooking fat** targets the biggest hidden error in photo estimates.
- **The calorie equation only exists for male and female bodies.** An "In between" average is simple and honest, and after 3 to 4 weeks your weight trend matters more than the starting equation.
- **The current activity numbers are low** compared with EU and UN reference values, so "Maintain" may lead to slow weight loss for many people (inferred).
- **Protein should be set per kilo of body weight, not as a percentage**; percentages give too little when calories drop and far too much for large, active people.
- **How you split meals barely affects weight**; some protein in each main meal and not eating most food late in the evening are what the evidence supports.
- **Competitors charge about 35 to 50 EUR a year in most of Europe** (more in Germany), and AI photo logging is almost always a paid feature. The median health app turns about 3 % of downloads into paying users.
- **Selling on your own website avoids app store fees**; a merchant of record takes over EU VAT and refunds for about 5 to 6 % of an annual price (estimate).
- **Your reel download pipeline must stay personal.** Instagram's terms forbid automated collection, and covers, frames and transcripts can be protected by copyright.

## (f) Problems found in the current app

Each item: what you see, why it matters, proposed fix.

1. **Magnesium preset above the EU supplement limit.** You see "+ Magnesium 300 mg" among the supplement presets. The EU food safety authority's safe upper limit for magnesium from supplements is 250 mg a day (magnesium in food does not count). Fix: change the preset to 250 mg and warn when a person's supplements add up to more.
2. **"Lower carb" macro preset has too much fat for EU guidance.** It sets 30 % protein, 45 % fat, 25 % carbohydrate; the EU reference range for fat is 20 to 35 % of energy. Fix: replace the three percentage presets with the protein-first styles of idea 5 and show a note when fat goes above 35 %.
3. **Activity numbers lower than reference values.** The four bands multiply resting energy by 1.25, 1.40, 1.55 and 1.75; EU values are 1.4, 1.6, 1.8 and 2.0, and the UN/WHO lowest "sedentary" value is 1.40. For a sample person this is about 230 kcal a day lower in the lowest band. Effect (inferred): "Maintain" may make many people slowly lose weight. Fix: decision 9.
4. **Sex handled inconsistently.** With only Male and Female this does not show today, but a third option would be treated as female in the calorie calculation and minimum, and as male for vitamin and mineral targets and in what Claude is told. Fix: one clear rule in all four places, as part of idea 2.
5. **Time stamps not ready for sync.** Every change is stamped with the device's local time including its time zone (+02:00 in summer, +01:00 in winter), and the app compares these stamps as text in two places (recent foods, and picking the latest saved review). Around a clock change or when travelling, the older change can look newer. Harmless today (inferred); with sync it would decide which edit wins. Fix: compare real moments in time, and let the server count changes itself.
6. **Restore replaces everything.** Restoring a backup wipes the diary and puts the backup in its place. Fine on one device; with sync, restoring an old backup could spread old data to every device. Fix: merge instead, when sync is built.
7. **GitHub Pages terms.** GitHub says Pages may not be used to run an online business or a commercial software service, and should not be used for passwords or card numbers. Fine for Stage 1. Fix: decisions 2 and 3 before the beta.
8. **Reel download pipeline and Instagram's terms.** The pipeline downloads reels automatically without logging in and keeps covers, frame sheets and transcripts. Instagram's terms forbid collecting information "in an automated way" without permission, and those images and texts can be protected by the creators' copyright. For your private use the practical risk is low (inferred), but it must not become a feature for other users. Fix: decision 6.
9. **Open Food Facts credit missing.** Barcode and product search results come from Open Food Facts, whose licence requires a credit with a link to openfoodfacts.org; the app shows the name above search results but no licence credit. Also, the browser sends barcodes and your internet address directly to Open Food Facts, which a privacy notice must mention. Fix: add "Product data from Open Food Facts (openfoodfacts.org), ODbL" wherever such data appears; later route lookups through the EU server.
10. **Claude key in the browser.** Fine for you (decision 4). For anyone else it is impossible: the key could be copied. Fix: the server in Stage 1.
11. **Photo evaluation is slow and heavy.** It sends a large image and asks for a long answer: about 0.017 USD per photo and roughly 10 seconds (estimates). Fix: the slim quick photo call of idea 1 (about half the cost and a few seconds, estimate).
12. **Chat sends a lot every time.** Each message resends up to 20 earlier messages plus your whole profile, pantry and recipe list, so cost grows as a conversation gets longer. Fix: decision 8.
13. **Percent-based protein can be far off.** For a 70 kg, very active person gaining weight, "Higher protein" gives about 4.1 g per kg, well above the useful 1.6 to 2.2 g. Fix: idea 5.
14. **No true "every other day" for supplements.** The schedule picks weekdays, so Monday, Wednesday, Friday, Sunday puts Sunday and Monday back to back; iron is best every other day. Fix: add an "every other day" option in the idea 3 plan.

## Supabase in Frankfurt plus a small sync keeps today's app intact

The current build keeps all data in the browser's IndexedDB (stores `kv`, `secrets`, `profileHistory`, `snapshots`, `records`, `recipes`, `media`), backs it up as one schema 2 JSON file to the user's private GitHub repo, and calls Claude straight from the browser with the user's key and the `anthropic-dangerous-direct-browser-access` header ([src/core.js](https://github.com/Prastako/nutrilog/blob/main/src/core.js); [src/backup.js](https://github.com/Prastako/nutrilog/blob/main/src/backup.js); [src/ai.js](https://github.com/Prastako/nutrilog/blob/main/src/ai.js)). **The data contract was written for sync from day one**: one envelope for every record, ULIDs that never change "so records can be merged between devices and apps", `updatedAt` where "the newer one wins in a merge", and tombstones "so a sync can see that it was removed" ([docs/DATA_FORMAT.md](https://github.com/Prastako/nutrilog/blob/main/docs/DATA_FORMAT.md)). That is why the public version does not need a rewrite.

Among the routes compared, **Supabase in an EU region with a hand-written push and pull sync on top of the existing store is the lowest-regret fit for a solo operator whose code is written by an AI**. Supabase's free plan covers 50,000 monthly active users and 500 MB of database but pauses after a week without activity; Pro costs 25 USD a month with 100,000 MAU, 8 GB disk, 7-day backups and a spend cap on by default, and extra users cost 0.00325 USD each ([Supabase pricing](https://supabase.com/pricing)). Frankfurt (`eu-central-1`) is one of six EU or European regions, though Supabase itself warns that "Region selection is a data-location control, not proof of regulatory compliance" ([Supabase regions](https://supabase.com/docs/guides/platform/regions)). The alternatives each fail on one axis: Firebase Authentication "processes data exclusively in the United States" ([Firebase privacy](https://firebase.google.com/support/privacy)); Dexie Cloud's hosted data sits in Azure US East ([Dexie Cloud GDPR](https://dexie.org/docs/cloud/gdpr-compliance)) and costs EUR 0.12 per user per month ([Dexie Cloud pricing](https://dexie.org/cloud/pricing)); Zero "does not support offline writes" by design ([Zero docs](https://zero.rocicorp.dev/docs/offline)), which rules it out for a diary used in shop basements or on flights; PocketBase is still pre-1.0 without backward compatibility guarantees ([PocketBase docs](https://pocketbase.io/docs/)); and Hetzner, the obvious own-server host, changed prices four times in 2026 alone ([Hetzner price adjustment](https://docs.hetzner.com/general/infrastructure-and-availability/price-adjustment/)), while an own server puts patches, TLS, backups and auth code on AI-maintained code without managed guard rails (inferred). PowerSync is the ready-made upgrade if hand-written sync becomes painful: its web SDK queues local writes and uploads them through your own connector, Pro starts at 49 USD a month, and free projects are deactivated after a week idle ([PowerSync pricing](https://www.powersync.com/pricing); [PowerSync JS Web SDK](https://docs.powersync.com/client-sdks/reference/javascript-web)).

The proposed protocol (inferred) is one Postgres table `records(user_id, id, type, schema, app, created_at, updated_at timestamptz, deleted, date, body jsonb, server_seq bigint)` with primary key `(user_id, id)` and Row Level Security `user_id = auth.uid()`. Push upserts only when the incoming `updated_at`, parsed as an instant, is newer, and stamps `server_seq` from a sequence; pull asks for rows with `server_seq` above the last seen value. This mirrors RxDB's Supabase pattern of a server-maintained `_modified` stamp and a `_deleted` soft-delete flag ([RxDB Supabase replication](https://rxdb.info/replication-supabase.html)), so RxDB or PowerSync can replace the hand-written code later without a data migration. Record-level last write wins is safe for append-mostly records (`food_entry`, `supplement_intake`, `body_weight`); the `profile` singleton should merge per section (`person`, `goals`, `food`, `kitchen`) with its own `updatedAt`, restore must switch from "replace" to "merge by id", `chat_message` and `photo_eval` can stay device-only at first, and the `media` store should not sync by default because 3 photos a day at 200 KB would add about 220 MB per premium user a year (all inferred or estimate). Hand-written sync is perhaps a few hundred lines on top of `recPut` and `markDirty` (estimate); the risk is subtle bugs, so the e2e suite needs two-browser scenarios (offline edits on both, delete on one, profile edits on both) before any outside user.

**The Claude key moves into a Supabase Edge Function** that checks the session, reads the user's monthly allowance, builds the prompt, calls Claude, records the `usage` numbers and returns the result. Edge Functions allow 150 s wall clock on Free and 400 s on paid plans with 2 s of actual CPU per request ([Supabase Edge Function limits](https://supabase.com/docs/guides/functions/limits)), which fits a 10 to 60 s photo evaluation because waiting on Claude uses no CPU (inferred). Free versus premium becomes a `plan` column read by the function, so premium is enforced on the server rather than by hiding buttons (inferred). Supabase recommends Upstash Redis only for burst rate limits keyed on the user id ([Supabase rate limiting](https://supabase.com/docs/guides/functions/examples/rate-limiting)); a `usage` row per user and month is enough for monthly quotas (inferred).

**Accounts** cost nothing inside the Supabase plan, but the built-in email sender is limited to "2 messages per hour", reaches only project team members and is "best-effort only", so any outside tester needs custom SMTP ([Supabase custom SMTP](https://supabase.com/docs/guides/auth/auth-smtp)); Resend's free tier sends 3,000 emails a month ([Resend pricing](https://resend.com/pricing)). Supabase passkeys entered beta on 28 May 2026 as "experimental and the API may change without notice" ([Supabase passkeys changelog](https://supabase.com/changelog/46458-passkeys-for-supabase-auth-beta)). Six-digit codes are preferred over magic links for an installed PWA because a link opens in the default browser rather than the installed window (inferred, not verified against vendor docs). Passkeys and Google sign-in bind to the domain, which is one more reason to fix the domain first.

**GitHub Pages must be left before the beta.** Its limits page says it "is not intended for or allowed to be used as a free web-hosting service to run your online business ... or any other website that is primarily directed at ... providing commercial software as a service (SaaS)" and "shouldn't be used for sensitive transactions like sending passwords" ([GitHub Pages limits](https://docs.github.com/en/pages/getting-started-with-github-pages/github-pages-limits)). Cloudflare Pages' free plan allows 500 builds a month with no bandwidth or commercial restriction on its limits page ([Cloudflare Pages limits](https://developers.cloudflare.com/pages/platform/limits/)); Vercel's Hobby plan is "restricted to non-commercial personal use only" ([Vercel fair use](https://vercel.com/docs/limits/fair-use-guidelines)); Netlify's free 300 credits at 15 credits per production deploy allow about 20 deploys a month ([Netlify pricing](https://www.netlify.com/pricing/)), too few for NutriLog's many daily pushes (inferred). The push-to-main workflow can continue by connecting Cloudflare Pages to the same repo (inferred).

**Distribution stays a PWA.** WebKit may evict a site's storage under pressure or after a period without interaction, and grants persistence by heuristics that favour Home Screen web apps ([WebKit storage policy](https://webkit.org/blog/14403/updates-to-storage-policy/)); with server sync, eviction only costs a re-download (inferred). A secondary source reports that iOS 26 opens Home Screen sites as web apps by default and that web push has worked since iOS 16.4 ([MobiLoud, secondary](https://www.mobiloud.com/blog/progressive-web-apps-ios/)). A Google Play Trusted Web Activity needs Digital Asset Links at the domain root ([Android TWA guide](https://developer.android.com/develop/ui/views/layout/webapps/guide-trusted-web-activities-version2)), which a `github.io/nutrilog/` project site cannot serve (inferred), and new personal developer accounts must run a closed test with 12 testers for 14 days ([Play Console Help](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/14151465?hl=en)). An iOS wrapper risks rejection under guideline 4.2 unless it adds native value ([MobiLoud, secondary](https://www.mobiloud.com/blog/app-store-review-guidelines-webview-wrapper/)).

**What carries over** (inferred from the code): all screens and strings, the one-file build, both e2e tests, the static `data/foods.json` served by the service worker, the recipe archive record format, and the schema 2 export (which becomes both the migration path and the GDPR "download my data" file). **What must change**: the `secrets` store gives way to a login session, `ai.js` calls the server instead of `api.anthropic.com`, `trackUsage` and the budget prompt move to the server, `applyPayload()` merges instead of clearing, and a sign-in screen, account deletion and export are added. The flat-rate paušální daň band I was cut to **9,162 CZK a month from 1 July 2026**, retroactive to January ([Finanční správa FAQ](https://app.fs.gov.cz/dotazy-verejnosti/dotaz/zmena-pausalnich-zaloh-pro-prvni-pasmo-od-1-7-2026-4578)). As a secondary activity alongside employment, no minimum social insurance advance is due until the tax base reaches 117,521 CZK a year ([ČSSZ 2026](https://www.cssz.gov.cz/-/prehled-nejdulezitejsich-udaju-pro-socialni-zabezpeceni-v-roce-2026)), which suits a slow start (inferred). Because domestic turnover now excludes supplies with place of supply abroad, income from a foreign merchant of record might not count toward the Czech threshold; this needs an accountant (inferred).

## Health-data rules shape the product more than any feature

**Nearly everything NutriLog stores should be treated as GDPR Article 9 health data.** The Article 29 Working Party's annex on apps lists "data on allergies disclosed to private entities" as health data, says weight "measured over time, especially in combination with age and sex, may be used to determine a significant aspect of an individual's health", names "apps to track food and exercise in an effort to lose weight" as apps where health data can be inferred, and holds that conclusions about health status are health data "regardless of their reliability" ([WP29 annex](https://ec.europa.eu/justice/article-29/documentation/other-document/files/2015/20150205_letter_art29wp_ec_health_data_after_plenary_annex_en.pdf)). The CJEU widened the inference line in OT (C-184/20), where the mere possibility of inferring a special category suffices ([Inside Privacy](https://www.insideprivacy.com/eu-data-protection/special-category-data-by-inference-cjeu-significantly-expands-the-scope-of-article-9-gdpr/)), and in Lindenapotheke (C-21/23), where orders of non-prescription pharmacy products were health data and competitors were allowed to sue over GDPR breaches ([Taylor Wessing](https://www.taylorwessing.com/en/insights-and-events/insights/2024/10/ecj-lindenapotheke)); supplement intake sits close to that fact pattern (inferred). The realistic basis is explicit consent under Article 9(2)(a) ([GDPR](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2016/679/oj)); the Digital Omnibus proposal to narrow the definition is still under Council negotiation in September 2026 with no adopted position ([Privacy Next](https://www.privacynext.eu/resources/digital-omnibus-gdpr-negotiations-at-the-council-september-2026-update/)). The same annex notes that processing only on the user's device falls outside data protection law for the user, so **today's design (data in the browser, the user's own key, backup to the user's own repo) is the lowest-risk model, and Jan becomes controller of health data the moment diaries reach his server or calls use his key** (inferred). Religious diet options (halal, kosher) reveal religious belief, another Article 9 category, so the consent text must cover them (inferred).

**Operations.** Records of processing are mandatory because the small-organisation exemption in Article 30(5) does not apply to special categories ([GDPR](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2016/679/oj)). The Czech authority's DPIA method requires an assessment when two or more characteristics are critical, lists health data among them, rates new technical solutions separately, and uses 10,001 data subjects as a large-scale heuristic ([ÚOOÚ DPIA list](https://uoou.gov.cz/media/profesional/seznam-operaci-zpracovani-nepodlehajicich-pozadavku-na-dpia.pdf)); a DPIA before the beta is the safe course and doubles as the lawyer's design document (inferred). Czech law sets the digital consent age at 15 ([Act 110/2019](https://e-sbirka.gov.cz/sb/2019/110/2019-04-24)) and has required opt-in for non-essential cookies and similar storage since 2022 ([epravo.cz](https://www.epravo.cz/top/clanky/cookies-nove-jen-se-souhlasem-poslanecka-snemovna-prijala-zprisnujici-pravidla-od-roku-2022-113207.html)). For transfers to Anthropic, the EU-US Data Privacy Framework survived the General Court on 3 September 2025 ([IAPP](https://iapp.org/news/a/european-general-court-dismisses-latombe-challenge-upholds-eu-us-data-privacy-framework)) but is under appeal as C-703/25 P ([WilmerHale](https://www.wilmerhale.com/en/insights/blogs/wilmerhale-privacy-and-cybersecurity-law/20251201-european-court-of-justice-to-review-challenge-to-eu-us-data-privacy-framework)); Anthropic's DPA with standard contractual clauses "is automatically incorporated" into its Commercial Terms ([Anthropic Privacy Center, DPA](https://privacy.claude.com/en/articles/7996862-how-do-i-view-and-sign-your-data-processing-addendum-dpa)). Anthropic deletes API inputs and outputs "within 30 days", keeps flagged content up to 2 years ([Anthropic retention](https://privacy.claude.com/en/articles/7996866-how-long-do-you-store-my-organization-s-data)), and since 9 June 2026 retains prompts to Covered Models (including Fable 5 and 5.1) for 30 days even for zero-retention customers ([Anthropic Covered Models](https://privacy.claude.com/en/articles/15425996-data-retention-practices-for-covered-models)). Prompts should carry no name, email or account id, and photos should be stripped of EXIF location data (inferred).

**AI Act.** Article 50(1) applies from 2 August 2026: providers of AI systems that interact with people must inform them "from the start of the first interaction in a clear and distinguishable manner" ([European Commission FAQ](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/faqs/transparency-obligations-under-article-50-ai-act)). Offering Claude-based chat and photo features under the NutriLog name most likely makes Jan the provider (inferred). The Digital Omnibus on AI (Regulation 2026/1744) postponed high-risk obligations to December 2027 and August 2028 but left Article 50 in place, with an Article 50(2) machine-readable marking grace period to 2 December 2026 only for systems already on the market before 2 August 2026 ([Gibson Dunn](https://www.gibsondunn.com/eu-ai-act-omnibus-agreement-postponed-high-risk-deadlines-and-other-key-changes/)); how 50(2) applies to AI text shown inside an app is a lawyer question.

**Medical devices.** The MDR says software "intended for life-style and well-being purposes is not a medical device", but intended purpose includes "promotional or sales materials or statements", and software informing diagnostic or therapeutic decisions is at least class IIa under Rule 11 ([MDR](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2017/745/oj)); MDCG 2019-11 revision 1 (June 2025) broadened Rule 11 to software "which informs clinical prognosis or prediction" ([Emergo by UL](https://www.emergobyul.com/news/european-revision-primary-software-guidance-mdcg-2019-11-revision-1-small-changes-meaningful)). Class IIa needs a notified body, out of reach for one person (inferred). Words to avoid in the app, store listing and website: diagnose, detect, treat, therapy, cure, prevent (a disease), deficiency, clinical, patient, symptom, prescription, "doctor-approved"; safer: "below your reference intake", "estimate", "for general wellbeing" (inferred). **The supplement routine is the feature most exposed to MDR, food law and liability** (inferred).

**Food law.** Regulation 1924/2006 governs nutrition and health claims in commercial communications about foods ([European Commission](https://food.ec.europa.eu/food-safety/labelling-and-nutrition/nutrition-and-health-claims_en)), so risk rises sharply if NutriLog earns money from specific foods or supplements through affiliate links or partnerships (inferred). There are still no binding EU maximum levels for vitamins and minerals in supplements ([Care Europe](https://care-europe.com/blog/eu-food-supplement-maximum-vitamin-mineral-levels-harmonisation/)), which argues for conservative caps at EFSA upper limits.

**Copyright and Instagram.** The pipeline downloads reels with yt-dlp without login, then keeps captions, transcripts, an 8-frame sheet and a cover ([PIPELINE.md](https://github.com/Prastako/nutrilog/blob/main/tools/reels/PIPELINE.md)). Instagram's terms forbid "accessing or collecting information in an automated way without our express permission" ([Instagram Terms](https://www.facebook.com/help/instagram/581066165581870)); the CJEU's VCAST ruling excludes a commercial cloud service that makes private copies for users from the private copying exception ([VCAST judgment](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=CELEX:62016CJ0265)); and the Czech text and data mining exception lets copies be kept only "as long as necessary for the analysis" and yields to reservations ([§ 39c](https://krajta.slv.cz/2000/121/par_39c-odst_1)). Recipe facts (ingredients, quantities, steps rewritten in the service's own words) carry low risk, while videos, covers, frame sheets and verbatim transcripts do not (inferred). If users ever publish recipes, NutriLog becomes a hosting service under the Digital Services Act (not researched).

**Duty of care.** Calorie tracker users report more eating concern and dietary restraint, controlling for BMI ([Simpson and Mazzeo 2017](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28214452/)). Czech Civil Code § 2898 voids clauses that exclude or limit liability for harm to health ([§ 2898](https://www.kurzy.cz/zakony/89-2012-obcansky-zakonik/paragraf-2898/)), and the new Product Liability Directive treats software, including SaaS, as a product with strict liability for personal injury "including medically recognised damage to psychological health" and for loss of personal data, for products placed on the market after 9 December 2026 ([Directive 2024/2853](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/dir/2024/2853/oj/eng)). Disclaimers frame the wellness purpose but do not remove liability; an 18+ limit, calorie floors, a weekly loss cap, no fat-loss goal at low BMI, no streaks for restriction, an option to hide numbers, and never calling a meal "allergen-free" are the practical answer (inferred).

## One energy engine with two faces covers ideas 2 and 4

Today `computeTargets` uses Mifflin-St Jeor (MSJ) resting energy `10*kg + 6.25*cm - 5*age + (male ? 5 : -161)`, multiplies it by 1.25, 1.40, 1.55 or 1.75, widens it into a range from a 10 % equation uncertainty and a 13 to 16 % activity uncertainty, applies lose 0.90, maintain 1.00 or gain 1.07, and floors it at max(resting energy, 1,500 kcal male or 1,200 otherwise) ([src/core.js](https://github.com/Prastako/nutrilog/blob/main/src/core.js)). **MSJ remains the best single equation for healthy adults when only weight, height, age and sex are known**: a systematic review found it within 10 % of measured resting energy for 82 % of non-obese and 70 % of obese people ([Frankenfield et al. 2005](https://www.jandonline.org/article/S0002-8223(05)00149-5/abstract)), and in adults with overweight or obesity Henry, Ravussin and MSJ all reached 73 % ([Clin Nutr ESPEN 2024](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38220405/)). EFSA used Henry equations "for practical reasons" without showing they are more accurate ([EFSA energy opinion](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13159830/)), so keeping MSJ is defensible (inferred). A fat-free-mass equation needs no sex term: Cunningham (22 x fat-free mass + 500) was within 10 % for 84.9 % of trained men and 78.4 % of trained women with measured body composition ([ten Haaf and Weijs 2014](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4183531/)), but body composition did not help in sedentary middle-aged adults ([Amaro-Gahete 2018](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6266118/)).

**Sex options.** MacroFactor's evidence summary says "differences in fat-free mass can fully explain sex differences in basal metabolic rates" ([MacroFactor](https://macrofactor.com/sex-basal-metabolic-rate/)). Major apps still calculate with a binary equation: MyFitnessPal advises sex assigned at birth before hormone therapy and says that after longer than a few months metabolism "might be closer to your gender identity" ([MyFitnessPal Help](https://support.myfitnesspal.com/hc/en-us/articles/360059617352-I-identify-as-transgender-non-binary-or-gender-non-conforming-Which-sex-should-I-select)); Cronometer uses profile sex for expenditure ([Cronometer forum](https://forums.cronometer.com/discussion/4147/gender)); Lose It! added Non-Binary and Prefer Not to Say in 2021 without saying which equation they map to ([PR Newswire](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/lose-it-sets-new-standards-as-first-weight-management-app-to-offer-gender-inclusivity-in-profile-settings-introduces-non-binary-or-prefer-not-to-say-preferences-301299406.html)). The Academy of Nutrition and Dietetics lists three acceptable approaches, including "Using a range with both male and female values" ([eatright.org](https://www.eatright.org/health/wellness/healthful-habits/how-to-approach-sex-specific-nutrition-recommendations-for-transgender-people)), and a 2026 review found first-year hormone therapy shifts of about +5 kg lean mass in trans men and -2.4 kg in trans women, with "Energy requirements should be reassessed longitudinally" ([Tosi et al. 2026](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13304584/)). No study has measured resting energy in trans or non-binary adults comparing MSJ inputs.

The proposed calculation basis (inferred) for a worked example of 30 years, 170 cm, 70 kg:

The app should never store hormone therapy status, which is health data under Article 9 ([GDPR Art. 9](https://gdpr-info.eu/art-9-gdpr/)); the helper text can mention it without recording it (inferred). Iron is the biggest sex-dependent micronutrient in `referenceValues()` (16 versus 11 mg) and is about menstrual losses, so an optional "Do you have periods?" question fits better than identity (inferred). The fix in code is one explicit rule in `computeTargets` (MSJ term, `FLOOR_ABS`), `referenceValues()` (currently non-female means male) and `src/ai.js` line 207 (currently non-female means "male physiology").

**Activity.** FAO/WHO/UNU put sedentary lifestyles at PAL 1.40 to 1.69, active at 1.70 to 1.99 and vigorous at 2.00 to 2.40, and one hour a day of moderate to vigorous exercise raises PAL by about 0.2 ([FAO/WHO/UNU 2004](https://www.fao.org/4/y5686e/y5686e07.htm)); EFSA uses 1.4, 1.6, 1.8 and 2.0 ([EFSA energy opinion](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13159830/)); doubly labelled water shows an adult average of 1.70 to 1.77 and 1.1 to 1.2 as essentially no activity ([Westerterp 2013](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3636460/)). **NutriLog's lowest band (1.25) sits below the FAO sedentary floor**, about 230 kcal a day under PAL 1.40 for the worked example (estimate); MSJ measures resting rather than basal energy, which justifies a slightly lower multiplier but not this much (inferred). Steps alone predicted PAL poorly in a doubly labelled water study ([BMC Research Notes 2011](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC3245484/)); walking costs about 0.53 kcal per kg per km ([Tucker et al. 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11101323/)), roughly 26 kcal per 1,000 steps at 70 kg (estimate), so steps should only nudge or check the base level. Training energy uses Compendium MET values (resistance training 3.5, vigorous lifting 6.0, moderate cycling 5.0, vigorous cycling 9.0, circuits 7.5, HIIT 11.0) ([2024 Compendium](https://pacompendium.com/conditioning-exercise/)) in the formula (MET - 1) x kg x hours x sessions x attendance / 7, which for 70 kg and three 60-minute sessions gives about 75 kcal a day for moderate strength training and 150 for vigorous lifting and agrees with the FAO +0.2 PAL rule (estimate). The proposed Fine-tune base levels are 1.40 (mostly sitting, under about 5,000 steps), 1.55 (sitting but walks a fair bit), 1.70 (on feet much of the day) and 1.90 (physically hard work), with attendance factors 0.9, 0.75 and 0.5 (design choices without evidence for the exact numbers). The four simple bands should be presets of this same model so switching views never makes the number jump, and more detailed input should not by itself narrow the displayed range (inferred).

**Recomposition** needs resistance training and a small deficit: a meta-analysis found a deficit of about 500 kcal a day prevented lean mass gains ([Murphy and Koehler 2022](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34623696/)), so the goal should stay at -5 to -10 %, never more than 500 kcal below expenditure, require at least two strength sessions and suggest higher protein (inferred).

**Weight-trend correction** is what actually narrows the range. MacroFactor reports that after 3 to 4 weeks its intake-and-weight estimate has a median error of about 135 kcal a day and beats a standard equation for 94.1 % of users, after excluding people with signs of partial logging (vendor-published, not peer reviewed) ([MacroFactor accuracy](https://macrofactor.com/algorithm-accuracy/)). The 7,700 kcal per kg rule is fine inside a 3 to 4 week window but badly overstates long-term loss; for long-run projections, each 24 kcal a day change in intake eventually moves weight about 1 kg ([Hall et al. 2011](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21872751/)). A 1 kg error in the trend spread over 28 days is about 275 kcal a day of expenditure error (estimate), which is why smoothing, a 3 to 4 week window, flagging days below half of the week's other days, and blending from 0 % data under 7 days to mostly data by 28 days are proposed (inferred). The weight trend is also the fairest answer for trans and non-binary users, because after a few weeks the sex input no longer drives the number (inferred).

## Protein per kilogram, not percentages, should drive ideas 3, 5 and 7

**Goals should be defined by a rate of weight change plus protein per kilo.** The ISSN position stand says slower loss preserves lean mass better in leaner people and cites 0.5 to 1.0 % of body weight a week for contest preparation ([ISSN diets and body composition](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5470183/)); lean gain works best from a 10 to 20 % surplus aiming at 0.25 to 0.5 % a week ([Iraki et al. 2019](https://www.mdpi.com/2075-4663/7/7/154)). EFSA's protein reference is 0.83 g per kg for all adults ([EFSA protein opinion](https://efsa.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.2903/j.efsa.2012.2557)); 1.4 to 2.0 g per kg "is sufficient for most exercising individuals" and 2.3 to 3.1 g may help lean trained people in a deficit ([ISSN protein](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5477153/)); gains plateau at about 1.62 g per kg with an upper confidence bound near 2.2 ([Morton et al. 2018](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28698222/)); people over 65 need at least 1.0 to 1.2 g per kg, except severe kidney disease ([PROT-AGE](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23867520/)). WHO anchors the health focuses: free sugars under 10 % of energy, salt under 5 g, at least 400 g of vegetables and fruit and 25 g of fibre a day ([WHO healthy diet](https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/healthy-diet)).

| Goal (simple view) | Energy | Protein, g per kg body weight (inferred mapping) |
|---|---|---|
| Stay at my weight and eat well | estimated expenditure | at least 0.83, default 1.0 to 1.2 |
| Lose fat, Gentle / Steady / Faster | -0.25 / -0.5 / -0.75 to 1.0 % of body weight a week (Faster only with clearly high body fat) | 1.2 to 1.6; 1.6 to 2.2 with strength training |
| Build muscle | +10 % (Gentle) to 15 to 20 % (Faster) | 1.6, range 1.6 to 2.2 |
| Lose fat and build muscle | maintenance or about -0.25 % a week | 2.0 to 2.4 |
| Fuel my training | expenditure including training | 1.2 to 2.0 |

For comparison, today's "Lose slowly" is a Gentle pace (about 0.3 % a week for an 80 kg man) and "Gain slowly" (+7 %) sits below the 10 to 20 % range, so the app cannot yet offer a steady loss pace, recomposition or a performance goal (estimate). Product-safety gates (inferred, not sourced thresholds): no fat-loss goal below BMI 18.5, no Faster pace in pregnancy, breastfeeding, under 18 or below BMI 25, protein computed from the weight at BMI 25 when BMI is above 30, and a one-line eating disorder note before setting a deficit. The Fine-tune pill groups are A body and weight, B training, C nutrient coverage (EFSA covers energy, macronutrients, 14 vitamins and 15 minerals ([EFSA DRVs](https://www.efsa.europa.eu/en/topics/topic/dietary-reference-values))), D health focus, and E life stage and safety flags; "More energy", "Better sleep" and "Skin" have no numeric target and should stay soft hints (inferred).

**Macros (idea 5).** EFSA's adult reference ranges are fat 20 to 35 % and carbohydrate 45 to 60 % of energy ([EFSA fats opinion](https://efsa.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.2903/j.efsa.2010.1461); [European DRVs summary](https://med.libretexts.org/Bookshelves/Nutrition/Principles_of_Nutritional_Assessment_3e_(Gibson_et_al.)/05:_Nutrient_reference_values_(Chapter_8a)/5.06:_European_Dietary_Reference_Values_(8a.6))), so today's Lower carb preset (fat 45 %) sits outside the EU range. ISSN finds no fat-loss advantage for lower-carbohydrate or ketogenic diets when protein and calories are matched ([ISSN diets](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5470183/)), which makes carbs versus fat a preference. MacroFactor sets protein first from preferences and training, then distributes the rest by style, with Low-carb capping carbohydrate at about 30 % of calories ([MacroFactor programs](https://help.macrofactorapp.com/en/articles/34-what-are-the-different-program-options-in-coached-mode)). Percentages mislead in both directions: for a 25-year-old, 185 cm, 70 kg, very active man gaining weight (target about 3,250 kcal), "Higher protein" 35 % yields about 284 g, or 4.1 g per kg, and the same percentage gives less protein exactly when calories drop for fat loss (estimate). Proposed algorithm (inferred): protein = g per kg from the goal table times reference weight, clamped to at least 0.83 g per kg and at most about 35 % of energy; fat = style share of energy with a floor of 20 % (and an unsourced 0.6 g per kg guard); carbohydrate = the remainder, with a plain note if it falls under 45 %. Styles: Balanced (fat 30 %), More carbs (fat 25 %, suggested for Fuel my training or 3+ training days), Less carbs (carbohydrate capped at about 30 %), Keto and Manual in Fine-tune only. The worked example for the "Why these numbers?" sheet: 30 years, 180 cm, 80 kg, three sessions a week, Lose fat Steady gives about 2,760 kcal expenditure, a 440 kcal deficit to 2,320 kcal, protein 160 g (2 g per kg, 640 kcal), fat 77 g (30 %), carbohydrate about 246 g, or 28, 30 and 42 % (estimate).

**Meal split (idea 7).** Protein per meal of about 0.25 g per kg or 20 to 40 g, spread every 3 to 4 hours, is the best-supported rule ([ISSN protein](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5477153/)), with 0.4 g per kg across at least four meals to maximise muscle building ([Schoenfeld and Aragon 2018](https://academicworks.cuny.edu/le_pubs/212/)). In a randomised crossover trial, morning-loaded and evening-loaded diets produced the same weight loss, but a big breakfast reduced hunger through the day ([Ruddick-Collins 2022](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36087576/)); eating most calories after 20:00 is associated with higher BMI in mostly observational data ([Meal timing review 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10995378/)). The four proposed patterns are 3 meals (30, 35, 35), 3 meals and a snack (25, 35, 10, 30; today's default), Big breakfast, light dinner (35, 35, 10, 20), and Light breakfast, main meal later (15, 35, 15, 35), each showing the user's kcal and protein per meal; protein should use a per-main-meal minimum (default 0.3 g per kg) rather than scaling with the kcal share, and the snack takes the remainder (inferred). With the worked example and "3 meals and a snack" that is breakfast about 580 kcal, lunch 810, snack 230 and dinner 700, with at least 30 g protein per main meal (estimate).

## A four-layer profile removes every overlap in ideas 6 and 8

Today's overlaps are concrete: "Build muscle" and "Lose fat" aims duplicate the direction setting; the "More protein" aim, the "High protein" diet style and the "Higher protein" macro preset express one idea three times; "Lower carb" is both a diet style and a macro preset; "Gluten free" and "Lactose free" styles overlap the cereal and milk allergen chips; and "Vegetarian" and "Vegan" overlap refusals of meat, fish, eggs and milk ([src/core.js](https://github.com/Prastako/nutrilog/blob/main/src/core.js); [src/strings.js](https://github.com/Prastako/nutrilog/blob/main/src/strings.js)). Mealime shows the cleaner pattern of three separate steps, diet type, allergies and restrictions, and dislikes ([Mealime allergies](https://support.mealime.com/article/59-choose-your-allergies-and-restrictions)), and MacroFactor keeps macro style apart from food choices ([MacroFactor programs](https://help.macrofactorapp.com/en/articles/34-what-are-the-different-program-options-in-coached-mode)).

The proposed structure (inferred) has four layers with one job each. Layer 1, eating pattern, is a single choice that excludes food groups (Everything, Little meat, Fish no meat, Vegetarian with advanced "no eggs" and "no milk products" toggles, Vegan, Carnivore with a "with milk products" toggle; Paleo later in Fine-tune). Layer 2, health and allergies, is hard and safety-critical: the EU 14 allergens plus coeliac disease (strict gluten ban), lactose intolerance graded by EFSA's finding that most people with lactose maldigestion tolerate up to 12 g in one dose ([EFSA lactose opinion](https://efsa.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.2903/j.efsa.2010.1777)), and kidney disease (caps protein, blocks high-protein goals); a one-line note explains that milk allergy is not lactose intolerance. Layer 3, religious and cultural rules (halal, kosher, no pork, no beef, no alcohol), is hard, with certification shown as a note because the ingredient checker cannot see it. Layer 4, preferences, is soft: dislikes, cuisines, "avoid gluten by choice", "prefer lactose-free products". De-duplication rules: a pattern shows implied exclusions as already covered, milk allergy hides the lactose question, coeliac implies the gluten cereals chip, a refusal equal to a pattern offers to switch the pattern, and one merged exclusion list carries a `source` field (pattern, allergy, condition, religion, refusal, dislike) so hard versus soft and the explanation come from the source. Today's chips map as follows: `ds_omnivore`, `ds_flexitarian`, `ds_pescatarian`, `ds_vegetarian`, `ds_vegan` to Layer 1; `ds_mediterranean` to health focus; `ds_lowcarb` and `ds_highprotein` to macro style and goal; `ds_glutenfree` and `ds_lactosefree` to Layer 2 or Layer 4; `aim_fatloss` and `aim_muscle` to goal group A; `aim_protein` dropped; `aim_fibre`, `aim_lesssugar`, `aim_lesssalt`, `aim_veg`, `aim_heart`, `aim_digestion` to health focus; `aim_energy`, `aim_sleep`, `aim_skin` to soft hints.

**Carnivore can be offered with an honest warning.** A modelling study found carnivore meal plans short on thiamin, with magnesium at 44 to 51 % of the reference, calcium at 74 to 84 %, vitamin C under 1.3 mg against 45 mg, fibre under 1 % of the target and saturated fat at 25.5 to 31.7 % of energy ([Goedeke et al. 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11722875/)); a self-selected survey of 2,029 carnivore dieters reported high satisfaction but a median LDL of about 172 mg/dL with no control group ([Lennerz et al. 2021](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2475299122106086)). The proposed card (inferred): "Carnivore means eating only animal foods. There are few long-term studies. People who eat this way often see LDL cholesterol go up, and without plants the diet is very low in fibre and vitamin C and often short in calcium, magnesium, thiamin, folate and iodine. If you choose it, have your cholesterol checked by your doctor, and use the nutrient view to see what is missing." The app should not nag daily but keep the nutrient view honest and ask for extra confirmation when combined with pregnancy, under-18 or kidney disease flags.

**Simple and Fine-tune.** Nielsen Norman Group finds progressive disclosure improves learnability and error rates, and that "Designs that go beyond 2 disclosure levels typically have low usability" ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/progressive-disclosure/)). The first-run flow should be a staged wizard of about six screens (main goal, pace, body basics, activity, what you eat, anything you must not eat) ending on a result screen with "Why these numbers?", and each profile section afterwards should show the simple answer plus one "Fine-tune" link; both views share one data model, and choices that cannot be shown simply appear as "Custom" rather than being dropped (inferred). Warnings stay inline and non-blocking except for safety combinations such as pregnancy plus deficit, kidney disease plus high protein, and BMI under 18.5 plus fat loss (inferred).

## The supplement plan should track freely and suggest rarely (idea 3)

Only a few timing rules have solid evidence. **Iron** works best on an empty stomach, 30 minutes before or 2 hours after food, and 2 hours away from tea, coffee, eggs and dairy ([NHS ferrous sulfate](https://www.nhs.uk/medicines/ferrous-sulfate/how-and-when-to-take-ferrous-sulfate/)); alternate-day dosing raised cumulative absorption from 16.3 % to 21.8 % in isotope studies ([Stoffel et al. 2017](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29032957/)) and, in a double-blind trial, gave equal ferritin with fewer side effects ([von Siebenthal et al. 2023](https://www.thelancet.com/journals/eclinm/article/PIIS2589-5370(23)00463-7/fulltext)). **Vitamin D** peaked 32 % higher with a fat-containing meal ([Dawson-Hughes et al.](https://www.jandonline.org/article/S2212-2672(14)01468-3/abstract)), and daily lower doses are preferred over intermittent large ones for adults 50+ ([Endocrine Society 2024](https://www.endocrine.org/clinical-practice-guidelines/vitamin-d-for-prevention-of-disease)), so the weekly plan means "a plan made once a week", not weekly boluses (inferred). Minerals need 4 hours from levothyroxine ([MedlinePlus levothyroxine](https://medlineplus.gov/druginfo/meds/a682461.html)) and 2 hours from quinolone antibiotics ([NHS ciprofloxacin](https://www.nhs.uk/medicines/ciprofloxacin/taking-or-using-ciprofloxacin-with-other-medicines-and-herbal-supplements/)). "Magnesium at night" and creatine timing are popular but weakly supported, so the app should present slots as convenience (inferred). Omega-3 above 1 g a day is associated with higher atrial fibrillation risk (RR 1.51) in cardiovascular trials ([Gencer et al. 2021](https://www.ahajournals.org/doi/10.1161/CIRCULATIONAHA.121.055654)); long-term zinc excess causes copper deficiency with possible lasting nerve damage ([Duncan 2023](https://bpspubs.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/bcp.15749)); about 1 g a day of vitamin C was associated with twice the kidney stone risk in Swedish men ([Thomas et al. 2013](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jamainternalmedicine/fullarticle/1568519)); and St John's wort "can interact in dangerous, sometimes life-threatening ways" with many medicines including the contraceptive pill ([NCCIH](https://www.nccih.nih.gov/health/st-johns-wort)).

EFSA's overview of upper levels (Version 11, August 2025) is the table to hard-code ([EFSA UL overview](https://www.efsa.europa.eu/sites/default/files/2024-05/ul-summary-report.pdf)):

**The current "Magnesium 300 mg" preset exceeds the 250 mg supplement UL** ([src/log.js line 482](https://github.com/Prastako/nutrilog/blob/main/src/log.js)); UK guidance calls up to 400 mg from supplements "unlikely to cause any harm" ([NHS other vitamins and minerals](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/vitamins-and-minerals/others/)), so the preset is not dangerous, but an EU app should follow EFSA (inferred). For iron and manganese the wording should be "above the level EFSA considers safe" because they have safe levels, not ULs (inferred).

**Regulatory status differs by country.** The Czech drug agency SÚKL treats melatonin as a medicine and ordered a "Melatonin 1 mg" product withdrawn in 2022 ([SÚKL](https://sukl.gov.cz/vyrobky-prerazene-do-skupiny-leciv/melatonin-1-mg-2/)); ashwagandha is banned in food in Denmark and was proposed in June 2024 by EU food agency heads for an EU-wide Article 8 procedure over reproduction, thyroid and liver concerns ([NutritionInsight](https://www.nutritioninsight.com/news/regulatory-update-spotlight-on-international-authorities-response-to-ashwagandha-safety-concerns.html)). **No major app proposes personalised supplement routines**: Apple Health tracks medications and supplements with day-of-week and cyclical schedules, checks interactions only in the US, and says it "should not be used as a substitute for professional medical judgment" ([Apple Support](https://support.apple.com/en-us/105064)); Cronometer logs supplements as foods with optional maximum thresholds ([Cronometer](https://support.cronometer.com/hc/en-us/articles/360000328566-How-do-I-add-a-supplement-to-my-diary)).

The proposed rule set (all inferred, for the lawyer to check before any suggestion reaches other users):

| Part | Rule |
|---|---|

| Tier 1, may be suggested | Vitamin D 10 to 25 µg daily (never above 50 µg from suggestions); omega-3 250 to 500 mg EPA+DHA (never above 1 g); B12 at label dose for vegan or vegetarian users; magnesium max 250 mg; calcium 500 mg per dose, evening, 2 h from iron; iodine 100 to 150 µg only if no iodised salt, dairy or fish; protein powder to fill a gap; creatine 3 to 5 g for strength goals (blocked with kidney disease) |

Proposed wording describes data, not the body, and offers rather than prescribes: "Your logged food gave on average 4 µg vitamin D a day over 14 days", "Would you like to add a vitamin D supplement to your weekly plan?", and the standing line "General information based on your food log, not medical advice. Check the label of your product." / "Obecné informace podle vašeho jídelníčku, nejde o lékařskou radu. Řiďte se etiketou výrobku." (inferred). The current schedule model stores weekdays only ([src/log.js](https://github.com/Prastako/nutrilog/blob/main/src/log.js)); a true every-other-day option needs a start date and a 2-day cycle, a small data change worth making before sync (inferred).

## Photo estimates miss by about a third, so the quick log must be quick to correct (idea 1)

**Weighed studies put general multimodal models at roughly 36 % error per meal.** With 52 standardised photos on a 24.3 cm plate beside known cutlery, weight error was 36.3 % for ChatGPT-4o and 37.3 % for Claude 3.5 Sonnet, energy error 35.8 % for both, with "systematic underestimation that increased with portion size" and catastrophic misreads such as scrambled eggs as pasta ([Fridolfsson et al. 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12513282/)). An NIH test presented in July 2026 (not yet peer reviewed) found MyFitnessPal, Lose It!, Cal AI and Appediet underestimated weighed meals by about 250 to 345 kcal and fat by about 30 g, worst for high-fat meals ([ScienceDaily](https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2026/07/260726015237.htm)); a Sydney study found butter missed on eggs on toast (-35 %) and bibimbap overestimated by 50 to 270 % ([Li et al. 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11314244/)). Model tier matters more than prompting: on packaged foods, Haiku's median energy error was 37.7 % against 19.3 % for Sonnet and 14.2 % for Opus (4.6 generation, per 100 g), Haiku "confabulated" a default 280 kcal for 17.7 % of items while Sonnet returned null when unsure, and chain-of-thought gave no gain ([Nakagawa and Yamamoto 2026](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13306177/)). Professional nutritionists average 41 % mass error on the Nutrition5k dishes, and overhead depth cut a model's calorie error from 26.1 % to 16.5 % ([Nutrition5k](https://arxiv.org/html/2103.03375)); SnapCalorie's claim of about 15 % relies on a depth sensor and has not been reproduced independently ([SnapCalorie FAQ](https://www.snapcalorie.com/faq.html)). A web report claiming 1.1 % error for one app is self-published with an invalid DOI and should be ignored ([DAI page](https://dietaryassessmentinitiative.org/publications/six-app-validation-study-2026/)). No weighed study of Sonnet 5 exists, and no study covers Czech sauce-heavy dishes; planning on plus or minus 30 to 40 % per meal, biased low, is prudent (inferred).

**The hand is a sensible reference only when measured and placed right.** No study has measured how much a hand, card or coin in frame improves an LLM's estimate, and known plate and cutlery did not rescue the Fridolfsson results ([Fridolfsson et al. 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12513282/)). Hand-based portion units correlated moderately to strongly with weighed intake in 1,110 Japanese adults ([Appetite 2025](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40669561/)), and MacroFactor tells users to shoot top down and include "your fist or a common object for scale" ([MacroFactor AI logging](https://macrofactor.com/ai-food-logging/)). Because a 10 % wrong hand length becomes about a 33 % volume error, the app should measure the hand once (ruler, or a photo next to a bank card) and pass the number as text, keep the hand flat on the table at plate distance, and also store the usual plate diameter, which is often the better reference at home (inferred). Human estimators judged rice and kimchi best at 45 degrees rather than overhead ([Choi and Kim 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12340097)), which suits Czech plated food with height (inferred). Browser access to phone depth sensors was not verified, so depth is treated as out of reach (inferred).

**One tap about cooking fat beats a better photo** for the largest error source (inferred from the fat findings above). The only study of LLM follow-up questions found no conclusive accuracy gain, partly because imperfect answers degraded estimates ([SnappyMeal](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.03907), per a search excerpt), so the question should be fixed chips ("Fat used in cooking: none / a little, about 1 tsp / normal, about 1 tbsp / a lot") whose kcal the app adds itself, asked only when the model returns a `hiddenFatLikely` flag (inferred).

**The confirm screen** follows the pattern of MyFitnessPal Meal Scan (confirm detected items, tap to adjust, "log your whole meal in one tap") ([MyFitnessPal Meal Scan](https://blog.myfitnesspal.com/meal-scan/)) and MacroFactor's editable Plate ([MacroFactor](https://macrofactor.com/ai-food-logging/)). Top to bottom (inferred): thumbnail and dish name with "Not this?"; 1 to 4 item rows with grams, tap to edit or remove, assumed cooking fat as its own row; one large kcal number rounded to 10 with a quiet asymmetric range (plus or minus 20 % for simple foods, 35 % for mixed or sauced dishes, wider upward for large or fatty plates); protein, carbs and fat only; the fat chips when flagged; "How much did you eat?" chips at 100 (preselected), 75, 50 and 25 % plus Other, because quarter steps match the real uncertainty better than a 10 % slider; one button naming the result, with slot from the existing `guessSlot()`; the verdict, reasons and suggestions below the button or behind "Why?". Uncertainty research supports this: numeric ranges caused "only a small decrease in trust", less than verbal hedges ([van der Bles et al. 2020](https://www.pnas.org/doi/full/10.1073/pnas.1913678117)). The entry should keep the original estimate, low and high bounds and the user's corrections, which are the data for a personal bias factor after 15 to 20 weighed meals (inferred).

**Image size and speed.** Plates and hands are large objects, so about 1092 x 819 px (1,170 tokens) should suffice for meals while labels keep 1568 px or more; this downscale is untested (inferred, token arithmetic from [Anthropic vision docs](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/vision)). Latency is mostly output: today's schema with two 16-nutrient blocks, reasons and suggestions (`max_tokens` 2,500) likely takes around 10 seconds, while a slim 150 to 250 token quick-log answer takes a few, so the fit evaluation should run separately in the background or on demand, and the upload should start the moment the photo is taken (estimates; Haiku's measured output speed is about 87 tokens a second ([Artificial Analysis](https://artificialanalysis.ai/models/claude-4-5-haiku)), Sonnet 5's was not measured). Model choice for this feature should follow a blind test on about 30 of Jan's weighed meals, Haiku 4.5 against Sonnet 5, given the Haiku confabulation finding (inferred).

## What remains uncertain

Several findings rest on thin or secondary evidence and should be rechecked before they drive a build. No weighed-portion study exists for Claude Sonnet 5 or for Central European dishes, and no study isolates the effect of a hand in the photo, so idea 1's accuracy must come from Jan's own test. MacroFactor's adaptive accuracy figures are vendor-published. The attendance factors, the "In between" floor of 1,350 kcal, the Fine-tune base PAL values, the fat guard of 0.6 g per kg, the keto cap and the BMI gates are design choices without direct evidence. The 1,200 and 1,500 kcal floors are industry conventions without a primary source in these notes. For supplements, the NIH fact sheets were unreachable, so claims about magnesium forms, calcium carbonate versus citrate and omega-3 absorption with meals remain unverified, and trigger thresholds for "gaps" in a food log have no published standard. On the business side, Stripe Managed Payments' acceptance of Czech sole traders, Paddle payout details, whether a personal Play account can join EEA external offers, domain prices, Czech VAT at 21 % and the CZK conversions (24 to 25 CZK per EUR, assumed) were not verified. Legally, the full MDCG 2019-11 revision 1 examples, the consolidated AI Act Article 50 after the Omnibus, Anthropic's presence on the DPF list, the current Commercial Terms on training, and the final Czech withdrawal-button law were not read in primary form. Usage profiles behind every Claude cost estimate are assumptions; real `usage` logs from the Stage 1 server function should replace them.

## Conclusion

The research reframes Jan's worry that "the current model will be hard to change": the parts that are genuinely hard to change are few and mostly not code (a domain, a data region, a sign-in provider, a legal entity, and promises about what is free), while the code that exists, down to the record format, was built in a way that survives the move. That shifts the sensible order of work: nutrition features first on the app Jan already uses, then the server proof on his own PC and phone, and only then outside users, with each hard choice made once at the last responsible moment before someone else's data depends on it. It also means the product's shape is set as much by law and by Claude's price list as by features: health-data consent, wellness-only wording and per-user cost ceilings are the difference between a service that can charge 40 EUR a year sustainably and one that loses money on its best customers.

Two design principles run through every nutrition idea and are worth keeping as house rules for future sessions. First, show honest ranges and let real data correct the starting guess: the weight trend overtakes the energy equation after a few weeks (which also makes the sex question matter less), and weighed meals calibrate photo estimates that otherwise read about a third low. Second, record and inform rather than prescribe: explain why a macro target is what it is, warn when supplements pass EU limits, but leave diagnosis and dosing to professionals. Built that way, NutriLog stays useful for Jan, fair to users of every body type, and on the safe side of the medical device line.
